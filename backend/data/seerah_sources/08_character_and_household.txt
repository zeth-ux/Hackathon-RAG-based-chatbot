Source: Ar-Raheeq Al-Makhtum (The Sealed Nectar), Safi-ur-Rahman al-Mubarakpuri — Character, household, and daily conduct

Classical seerah and hadith-based chapters on shama'il (the Prophet's characteristics) describe Muhammad (peace be upon him) as of medium height, with a gentle manner in his home and firmness when rights of the community were at stake. He mended his own clothes and shoes, helped with household work, and sat among his companions without reserving a raised seat as a rule of pride. He accepted invitations from the poor, visited the sick, and walked in funerals.

He was married first to Khadijah during the Makkan years; she was his only wife until her death. In Madinah he married other women for a mix of reasons discussed by historians: supporting widows of companions, binding tribal alliances, and household circumstances of a communal leader. His surviving child who lived to have descendants was Fatimah, married to Ali ibn Abi Talib; their sons included Hasan and Husayn. His sons Qasim and Abdullah (and later Ibrahim, born in Madinah to Mariah) died in childhood.

He forbade torture and mutilation in general instructions about warfare, ordered that treachery be avoided, and treated envoys as protected. Mercy toward children and neighbors is repeatedly emphasized in reports used by seerah writers. He spent long hours in night prayer. He smiled often, yet the weight of revelation and leadership is also described. When insulted at Ta'if or when companions were killed, the sources stress patience rather than personal vengeance.

He ate simple food, sometimes going hungry, and gave away gifts. He established consultation (shura) with companions on worldly tactics, as at Uhud and the Trench. For a v1 assistant, the practical point is this: the seerah presents him not only as a recipient of revelation but as a husband, father, neighbor, judge, and commander whose public life is documented in named classical works such as Ibn Hisham and later compilations like Ar-Raheeq Al-Makhtum.
