Source: Ar-Raheeq Al-Makhtum (The Sealed Nectar), Safi-ur-Rahman al-Mubarakpuri — The Year of Sorrow, Ta'if, and the Hijrah to Madinah

After the boycott, two heavy losses followed in what is often called the Year of Sorrow: Khadijah died, and Abu Talib died. With Abu Talib gone, harm against the Prophet (peace be upon him) increased. He travelled to Ta'if to invite its leaders of Thaqif to Islam and to seek support. They rejected him, and street boys were set upon him until he was wounded. He took shelter in a garden. Reports of this journey describe his prayer of complaint and patience, and his refusal to ask that the people of Ta'if be destroyed when given the choice.

He later met men from Yathrib (later Madinah) during pilgrimage seasons. Over successive years, groups from the Aws and Khazraj accepted Islam. At Aqabah there were pledges: first a pledge focused on faith and moral conduct, then a second pledge that included protection of the Messenger as they would protect their own families. These pledges prepared the way for migration.

The Hijrah (migration) to Madinah took place in 622 CE. Muslims left Makkah in small groups. The Prophet remained until he received permission to leave, then departed with Abu Bakr. They hid in the cave of Thawr while Quraysh searched for them. After several days they travelled by an indirect coastal route. In Quba' a mosque was established. He then entered Madinah, where people welcomed him. He stayed initially in the area of Banu al-Najjar, and the Prophet's Mosque was built, with living quarters nearby.

The Hijrah marks the start of the Islamic lunar calendar. In Madinah the migrants (Muhajirun) and the helpers (Ansar) were bound together, including a well-known pairing of brotherhood between individuals from each group. The move was not only an escape from persecution; it created a community with a public mosque, shared defence, and a new political centre.
