Source: Ar-Raheeq Al-Makhtum (The Sealed Nectar), Safi-ur-Rahman al-Mubarakpuri — Hudaybiyyah, the opening of Makkah, and the Farewell Pilgrimage

In 6 AH the Prophet (peace be upon him) set out with companions intending umrah, not war, and halted at al-Hudaybiyyah. Quraysh blocked entry to the sanctuary. Negotiations produced the Treaty of Hudaybiyyah. Terms included a ten-year truce, postponement of umrah until the following year, and a clause about returning some migrants from Makkah that many Muslims found painful. The treaty also allowed other tribes to ally with either side. Though it looked like a concession, it opened a period of easier preaching. The expedition to Khaybar followed in 7 AH. In 8 AH, after Quraysh allies violated the peace, the Muslims marched on Makkah.

The opening (fath) of Makkah in 8 AH (630 CE) was achieved with overwhelming numbers and relatively little fighting. The Prophet declared a general amnesty, with limited exceptions named in the sources. He entered the sanctuary, destroyed the idols in and around the Ka'bah, and recited that truth had come and falsehood had passed away. Many Quraysh accepted Islam, including figures who had been former enemies. Khalid ibn al-Walid and Amr ibn al-As had already become Muslim before this.

After Makkah came Hunayn and the siege of Ta'if, then a wider submission of tribes. In 9 AH delegations arrived in large numbers (the Year of Delegations). In 10 AH the Prophet performed the Farewell Pilgrimage. In the sermon at Arafah he emphasized the sanctity of life and property, the abolition of pre-Islamic blood vengeance practices in a new legal order, fairness, and that no Arab had superiority over a non-Arab except by piety. He indicated that he had conveyed the message. He died in Madinah in 11 AH (632 CE), in the house of Aishah, after a brief illness. He was buried in that room. Abu Bakr led the community thereafter.

The late seerah therefore moves from truce, to the peaceful-majority conquest of Makkah, to the Farewell Pilgrimage and the Prophet's death in Madinah.
