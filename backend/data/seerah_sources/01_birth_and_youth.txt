Source: Ar-Raheeq Al-Makhtum (The Sealed Nectar), Safi-ur-Rahman al-Mubarakpuri — Birth, childhood, and youth in Makkah

Muhammad ibn Abdullah ibn Abd al-Muttalib (peace be upon him) was born in Makkah in the Year of the Elephant, commonly dated to 570 CE. His father, Abdullah, died before his birth. His mother was Aminah bint Wahb. In keeping with Makkan custom of that time, he spent part of his early childhood in the desert with a wet-nurse from Banu Sa'd, Halimah bint Abi Dhu'ayb. After he was returned to Makkah, his mother Aminah died at al-Abwa' while travelling, when he was about six years old. His grandfather Abd al-Muttalib then cared for him until Abd al-Muttalib died, after which his uncle Abu Talib took him into his household.

As a young man he worked as a shepherd and later accompanied trading caravans. He became known among the Quraysh for honesty and trustworthiness and was called al-Amin (the trustworthy). When the Ka'bah was rebuilt after flood damage, the clans disputed who should replace the Black Stone. They accepted his proposal to place the stone on a cloth so that each clan could lift a corner, after which he set the stone in place himself.

Khadijah bint Khuwaylid, a respected merchant of Makkah, hired him to take her goods to Syria. She later proposed marriage. He was about twenty-five and she was older; they married, and the marriage is described as one of affection and support. Their children included Qasim, Zaynab, Ruqayyah, Umm Kulthum, Fatimah, and Abdullah (some of the sons died in childhood). Before prophethood he was known to withdraw for reflection, including periods of retreat in the cave of Hira on Jabal al-Nur near Makkah.

This summary follows the well-known narrative of his lineage, orphanhood, upbringing under Abd al-Muttalib and Abu Talib, reputation for trustworthiness, role in placing the Black Stone, and marriage to Khadijah as presented in Ar-Raheeq Al-Makhtum.
