Source: Ar-Raheeq Al-Makhtum (The Sealed Nectar), Safi-ur-Rahman al-Mubarakpuri — First revelation and the beginning of the call

When he was forty, during a retreat in the cave of Hira in Ramadan, the first revelation came. The angel Jibril (Gabriel) appeared and said "Iqra'" (Read). He replied that he was not one who reads. After the command was repeated, he was taught the opening verses of Surah al-Alaq: "Read in the name of your Lord who created..." He returned to Khadijah shaken. She reassured him and took him to her cousin Waraqah ibn Nawfal, who recognized the experience as akin to the namus (revelation) that had come to Moses and said that his people would oppose him.

For a time revelation paused (the period called fatrah), then resumed. The earliest followers included Khadijah, Ali ibn Abi Talib (then a youth in the household), Zayd ibn Harithah, and Abu Bakr. Abu Bakr in turn brought others among his close companions. The call was at first private, inviting relatives and trusted individuals to worship Allah alone and abandon idol worship.

After about three years the call became public. The Prophet (peace be upon him) was commanded to warn his nearest kindred and later to proclaim the message openly. He spoke against idolatry at the Ka'bah and called the Quraysh to tawhid (the oneness of God). Many of the Quraysh leadership rejected the message, seeing it as a threat to their religious prestige, tribal order, and the pilgrimage economy around the idols of the Ka'bah.

Early revelations emphasized the oneness of Allah, moral accountability, care for the poor and orphan, and the reality of resurrection. The first Muslims were a mix of the weak and the strong: some enslaved people such as Bilal ibn Rabah, some young members of notable families, and some established merchants. The beginning of the mission is therefore dated from the first revelation in Hira, followed by a quiet stage of invitation and then an open stage of preaching in Makkah.
